## Where a BeReal QR code actually makes sense

BeReal is not a platform where you are building a public brand or running ads. Most of its users are sharing with friends. So the use cases here are smaller and more personal than something like Instagram or LinkedIn.

**Printed friend group materials.** Think event flyers, house party posters, or a card you hand out at a camp or retreat. If someone wants to add you on BeReal after meeting in person, scanning a QR code is faster than asking them to search for your username.

**Personal portfolio or link pages.** Some people include BeReal alongside other social links on a personal site or a Linktree-style page. A QR code version of that link fits naturally on a printed portfolio or a zine.

**Stickers and physical merch.** It is a small niche, but people do print stickers with their social QR codes. A BeReal QR code on a laptop sticker or a notebook cover is one way to casually share the profile without making a big deal of it, which fits the app's general vibe.

## What this tool does vs BeReal's own sharing

BeReal has its own share feature built into the app. When you share your profile from inside BeReal, it generates a link you can copy and send through any messaging app. That covers most everyday situations.

This tool does something different: it converts that link into a downloadable image file. The QR code you generate here is a PNG or SVG you can print, embed in a document, put on a sticker, or use anywhere a link cannot go. BeReal's share button does not give you a file. This tool does.

## File format guide

Use **SVG** for print. It is a vector format and stays sharp at any size, from a business card to a poster.

Use **PNG at 300px or higher** for standard print materials if SVG is not an option in your software.

Use **PNG at 256px** for web embeds, message backgrounds, or digital sharing.

Do not use JPEG. JPEG compression softens the QR pattern edges and is the most common reason a printed code fails to scan.
