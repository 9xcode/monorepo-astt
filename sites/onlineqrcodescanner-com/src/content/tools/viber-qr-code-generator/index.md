## What this QR code does

When someone scans the QR code, their phone reads the Viber URL encoded in it. If they have Viber installed, the phone will prompt them to open the link inside the app. The destination depends on the link type:

- A **public account link** opens the public account page inside Viber, where the person can follow the account or send a message.
- A **community invite link** opens a screen prompting the person to join the community.

If Viber is not installed, the link opens in a browser and redirects to Viber's website or the Viber download page, depending on the device.

The QR code is static. The Viber link is encoded directly into the pattern at the moment you generate it. If your link changes later, you need to generate and reprint the QR code.

## Viber link formats and how they affect QR code quality

Not all Viber links are the same length, and that matters for printed QR codes.

A `vb.me` short link (around 20 to 25 characters) produces a very simple QR pattern with large individual modules. This scans fast and reliably even at small print sizes, around 1.5cm x 1.5cm on a business card.

A longer invite link with a hash produces a denser pattern. Dense patterns need to be printed larger to scan reliably. If you are working with a longer Viber URL, aim for at least 2.5cm x 2.5cm in print, and always test before ordering a print run.

If you have a long Viber URL and need to print it small, run it through a URL shortener first and encode the short link here instead.

## Static vs dynamic QR codes

This tool generates static QR codes. The Viber link is encoded directly into the QR pattern. Once you print it, the link cannot be changed without generating and reprinting a new code.

Dynamic QR codes from paid third-party services encode a redirect URL that you can update through a dashboard without reprinting. They also provide scan analytics.

For most Viber use cases, a static code is the right choice. Your Viber public account link or community invite link is not going to change often, and most people using this tool do not need scan tracking. If you do want analytics, paste your Viber link through a URL shortener with click tracking first, then encode the short link here instead of the Viber URL directly.

## File format guide for printing

Use **SVG** for anything that will be physically printed. SVG is a vector format and stays perfectly sharp at any size, from a small business card to a large event banner.

Use **PNG at 512px or higher** for standard print materials if your design software does not support SVG. For business cards, 512px is sufficient. For larger formats, use SVG.

Use **PNG at 256px to 300px** for digital use: website embeds, email newsletters, social media posts, or slide decks.

Do not use JPEG. JPEG compression blurs the sharp edges of QR modules, and that blur is the most common reason a printed QR code fails to scan. The QR pattern relies on hard edges between dark and light squares. Any lossy compression that softens those edges will cause scanning failures on some phone cameras.
