## What is a Systematic Investment Plan?

[Direct Answer]: A **Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)** is an investment method where you commit to investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals — typically monthly — into a mutual fund, index fund, or ULIP. Rather than investing a large lump sum at one point in time, a SIP automates your investing habit so wealth is built incrementally and consistently over years or decades.

The **Systematic Investment Plan Calculator** above helps you answer the single most important investment question: *"If I invest ₹X per month for Y years at Z% annual return, how much will I have?"*

---

## How Does a Systematic Investment Plan Work?

A SIP works in three simple steps:

1. **Choose a Mutual Fund:** Select a fund matching your risk appetite — equity funds for higher returns with more risk, or debt funds for stability.
2. **Set the SIP Amount & Date:** Decide how much money to invest each month and on which date. The amount is auto-debited from your bank account on that date every month.
3. **Stay Invested & Let Compounding Work:** The monthly amounts accumulate into units of the mutual fund at whatever price (NAV) they are on investment day. Over years, the returns compound exponentially.

## Systematic Investment Plan vs. SIP — What's the Difference?

Nothing — they are the same thing. **SIP** is simply the popular short-form abbreviation for **Systematic Investment Plan**. Both terms appear in financial literature, banking documents, and investment platforms interchangeably. This calculator works identically for both.

## The Power of Rupee Cost Averaging in an SIP

The foundational advantage of a Systematic Investment Plan over lump-sum investing is **Rupee Cost Averaging (RCA)**:

- When the market is **high**, your fixed monthly amount buys **fewer units** (you buy at a premium)
- When the market is **low**, your fixed monthly amount buys **more units** (you buy on sale)

Over the long run, this automatic buy-more-when-cheap effect mathematically lowers your average cost per unit compared to any single lump-sum investment. It removes the stress of having to "time the market."

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Is a Systematic Investment Plan the same as an SIP?

Yes, completely. "Systematic Investment Plan" is the full formal name; "SIP" is its abbreviation. Banks, mutual fund houses, and investment apps use both terms. There is no functional difference between them.

### What is a good SIP amount to start with?

There is no universally perfect amount — the best SIP amount is the largest you can comfortably invest every month without affecting your emergency fund or living expenses. Many funds allow starting with as low as ₹500 per month. The most important factor is consistency: a small SIP started today is always better than a large SIP planned for a future date that never arrives.

### What happens if I miss an SIP installment?

If an SIP installment fails (due to insufficient balance), most mutual funds treat it as a skipped month and continue the SIP next month without cancelling your plan. However, repeated failures may trigger SIP cancellation depending on your fund house's policy. Always maintain sufficient balance a day before your SIP date.

### Can I run two different SIPs simultaneously?

Yes. Most investors run multiple SIPs in parallel — for example, one SIP in a large-cap equity fund for stable growth and another in a mid-cap fund for higher return potential. Your bank and brokerage allow any number of parallel SIP mandates.

### Is a Systematic Investment Plan tax-free?

No. Returns from equity mutual fund SIPs held for over one year are taxed as **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)** at 10% beyond ₹1 lakh annually in India. Returns from SIPs redeemed within one year are taxed as **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)** at 15%. Tax-saving options like ELSS mutual funds allow SIPs that qualify for deductions under Section 80C.
